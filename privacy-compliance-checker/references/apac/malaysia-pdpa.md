# Malaysia — PDPA 2010 (as amended by the Amendment Act 2024)

## Snapshot

| | |
|---|---|
| Law | Personal Data Protection Act 2010, amended by the PDP (Amendment) Act 2024 |
| Amendments in force | Staged through 2025; the main tranche including breach notification and DPO duties from **1 June 2025** |
| Regulator | Personal Data Protection Commissioner (JPDP) |
| Scope | Personal data processed in respect of **commercial transactions** |
| Penalties | Raised by the 2024 amendment — fines up to RM1m and/or imprisonment |

## What the 2024 amendment changed

Worth knowing, because a policy written before 2025 will be visibly stale:

- "Data user" renamed **data controller**; **data processors now carry direct security duties**
- **Mandatory breach notification** to the Commissioner (as soon as practicable, within 72 hours)
  and to affected data subjects where there is significant harm
- **Mandatory DPO appointment** for controllers and processors meeting the Commissioner's
  thresholds, with DPO details to be notified to the Commissioner
- New **right to data portability**
- Cross-border transfer: the old "whitelist" mechanism was removed in favour of a
  substantially-similar-law or adequate-protection standard
- **Biometric data** added to sensitive personal data

## Browser-observable checklist

### The bilingual notice — s.7(2)
- [ ] The privacy notice is provided in **both Bahasa Malaysia and English**. This is an express
      statutory requirement, not best practice, and it is the single most commonly failed item on
      Malaysian sites. An English-only notice is non-compliant on its face
- [ ] Both versions are actually reachable — a language toggle that 404s or reverts counts as a fail
- [ ] Both versions carry the same substance

### Notice content — s.7
- [ ] Description of the personal data being collected
- [ ] Purposes of collection and further processing
- [ ] Source of the data where not collected directly
- [ ] The individual's right to request access and correction, and the contact for doing so
- [ ] Classes of third parties to whom data may be disclosed
- [ ] Whether supplying the data is **obligatory or voluntary**, and the consequences of not supplying
- [ ] Choices offered for limiting processing

### DPO
- [ ] **DPO contact details are published** and reachable

### Consent and rights
- [ ] Consent obtained and capable of being recorded
- [ ] Access, correction, and the ability to withdraw consent
- [ ] **Data portability** — new under the amendment; policies not mentioning it are likely stale
- [ ] Right to prevent processing for **direct marketing** purposes, honoured on request

### Cross-border
- [ ] Transfers addressed against the current adequate-protection standard, not the repealed
      whitelist. A policy still citing a "white list" of approved countries is out of date

### Breach
- [ ] Breach notification commitment present, reflecting the 2025 obligation

## Not observable from the browser

- Whether the DPO has been notified to the Commissioner
- Registration under the applicable class of data user, where required for the sector
- Security standards compliance, now extending directly to processors
- Retention and disposal practice

## Common failure modes

1. **English-only privacy notice** — a bare statutory breach, and easy to verify
2. A Bahasa Malaysia toggle that exists in the navigation but does not serve a translated notice
3. Policy still describing the pre-2025 regime: no breach notification, no portability, whitelist
   transfers, "data user" terminology
4. No published DPO contact
